# How long do x402 sellers last?

A survival study of one cohort, 2026-08-19 to 2026-11-17.

Every day I probe the same 1,521 hosts, the ones the CDP Bazaar listed on 2026-07-26, and sign the record. Most x402 counts are snapshots of a catalog that keeps changing, so they can't tell a seller that left from one that never arrived. This follows one group forward instead: the 1,348 hosts that were selling on 2026-08-19, meaning they answered with an x402 payment challenge. All 91 days read here verified against the published key.

## Findings

1. **About one host in five stopped selling for good: 286 of 1,348 (21.2%).** 1,021 (75.7%) still served a challenge on 2026-11-17. The other 41 are unsettled: quiet on the last day, but selling at some point in the last 14 days.

2. **Counted by operator, it's about one in seven: 125 of 866 (14.4%).** Hosts overstate the loss because a few operators run many hosts. 63 operators run more than one, 545 hosts between them. Two operators account for 122 of the 286 stopped hosts (42.7%): klymax402.com lost 78, 77 of them on 2026-09-18, and merit-systems.vercel.app previews lost 44 between 2026-09-01 and 2026-11-04.

3. **Operators stopped at about one in twenty per month, and the latest period was faster.**

- 2026-08-19 to 2026-09-17, 30 days: 45 of 866 operators stopped (5.2%), and 66 of 1,348 hosts (4.9%).
- 2026-09-18 to 2026-10-17, 30 days: 39 of 821 operators stopped (4.8%), and 133 of 1,282 hosts (10.4%).
- 2026-10-18 to 2026-11-04, 18 days (the latest period that can be read yet): 41 of 782 operators stopped (5.2%), and 87 of 1,149 hosts (7.6%).

The latest period is 18 days long, so a similar share in fewer days is a faster pace.

4. **Sellers that stop mostly vanish.** On 2026-11-17, of the 286 stopped hosts, 139 gave no answer at all, 104 answered 404 or 410, 19 returned a server error, and 3 answered with a success status without asking for payment. The other 21 answered with another status.

5. **The catalog listed temporary builds as sellers.** 54 of the 1,348 hosts were Vercel preview builds, the short-lived copies Vercel makes of one branch or commit, and 51 of them belonged to one team (merit-systems.vercel.app previews). A buyer that picks one of those is paying a build that is meant to disappear.

6. **Why 14 days counts as stopped.** Of 295 silences of 14 days or more, 9 ended with the host selling again (3.1%). The longest silence that ended in a comeback was 53 days. So a host counted as stopped here has a small chance of coming back, and the rate is stated rather than assumed.

## Method

- **Selling** means the day's verdict was OK, WARN, V1, NON_EVM or BLOCKED: an x402 challenge of any version or network.
- **The cohort starts on 2026-08-19** because the classifier changed that day and 54 hosts changed status overnight, against a median of 9 on ordinary days. An earlier start would count a change of method as sellers leaving. The two later changes, on 2026-09-14 and 2026-09-15, moved 3 and 6 hosts.
- **Stopped** means no challenge on any of the last 14 days. The stop date is the day after the last day selling. Stops can be dated only through 2026-11-04; a host that stopped after that can't be told apart from an outage yet.
- **Operator** means the registrable domain under the Mozilla Public Suffix List (the release of 2026-11-16, pinned by hash), so a.vercel.app and b.vercel.app are two operators while a.example.com and b.example.com are one. Vercel preview builds are grouped by the team in their name. An operator stopped when every one of its hosts stopped. One person can run several domains, so this is a floor on concentration, not a proof of who runs what.

## What this does not show

- A host that stopped serving a challenge at its pinned URL may be charging at another URL, under another domain, or through another catalog.
- Sellers that joined after the list was pinned are not in it, so this shows how one cohort ages, not whether the market is growing or shrinking.
- One probe a day from one datacenter vantage.

## Reproduce

```
python verify_snapshot.py --all
python survival.py --end 2026-11-17
```

survival_2026-11-17.json beside this file has every figure, its definition, the hash of every day read, and every stopped host with its operator, its last day selling and what it answers now.
