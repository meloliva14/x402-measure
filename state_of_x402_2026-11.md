# State of x402 deployments, 2026-11

Issue 1. This covers 2026-10-17 to 2026-11-16 (31 days) and compares it with 2026-09-17 to 2026-10-16 (30 days).

Every day I probe the same 1,521 hosts, the ones the CDP Bazaar listed on 2026-07-26, and sign the record. Every figure here is computed by a script from those signed records, and the script refuses to report on a day whose signature does not verify. All 92 days it read verified. The classifier was byte-identical on every day of both months.

State figures are read on the last day of each month: 2026-11-16, against 2026-10-16. On 2026-11-16, 1,350 of 1,521 hosts gave an answer of any kind (1,398 on 2026-10-16).

## Figures

1. **Payment-gated hosts: 1,051**, down 111 from 1,162. A host counts if it answered with an x402 challenge of any version or network (verdict OK, WARN, V1, NON_EVM or BLOCKED).

2. **Signable by a stock v2 client: 950 of 1,051 (90.4%)**, against 1,053 of 1,162 (90.6%). Signable means verdict OK: a stock @x402/evm 2.x buyer could build a payment from the challenge with no caveats. Hosts signable only with caveats (verdict WARN) are not counted: 0 this month, 1 before. 11 hosts served a v2 challenge it cannot sign (6 before).

3. **v1 only: 75 hosts**, down 12 from 87. These serve a v1 challenge in the body and a v2 client cannot read it at all. Separately, 231 signable hosts also serve a body the v1 client x402-fetch can read, so both generations of buyer can pay them (239 before).

4. **Stopped serving a challenge: 149 hosts** served one on at least one day of the prior month and on no day of this one. 30 of them answered with another HTTP status at least once; 119 gave no usable answer all month (no connection, a 5xx origin error, or a 429). **Started: 2**, a challenge this month after none the month before. In the prior month the same counts were 74 stopped and 5 started, measured against a baseline month (2026-08-17 to 2026-09-16) in which the classifier ran in 5 versions, so that pair is the less exact one.

5. **Price changes: 30 of 1,061 signable hosts** showed more than one price at their pinned URL during the month. Price means accepts[0].amount, and a host counts if it was signable on at least two days with the same asset. 12 ended the month higher than they started it, 15 lower, and 3 back where they started. Prior month: 25 of 1,127. 2 hosts switched asset during the month and are left out of this count.

6. **Median price: 0.02 USDC**, over the 934 signable hosts quoting the Base mainnet USDC contract on 2026-11-16 (0.02 USDC over 1,037 on 2026-10-16).

7. **Assets: 934 of 950 signable hosts quote the Base mainnet USDC contract, 14 quote the Base Sepolia testnet USDC contract, and 2 quote something else** (1,037, 13 and 3 before). The snapshot records the contract address, not the network, so the chain is read from the address. A testnet challenge is signable and moves no real money.

8. **Discovery manifests: 712 of the 972 hosts pinned as manifest publishers** answered /.well-known/x402 with a success status (768 before). That counts a response, not a valid manifest. Hosts serving a signature document: 0 on every day of the month, and 0 in the month before.

## What this does not show

- Wallets. The snapshots do not record payTo, so nothing here says whether a payout address moved or is shared. That needs a different instrument.
- Networks in general. Only the first accepts[] entry is classified, and for a signable host the network itself is not written down, only the asset. A host offering Solana second is invisible here.
- Hosts outside the pinned list. Hosts that joined the Bazaar after the list was pinned are not in this census, so started counts only hosts already on it.
- Intent. A host that stopped serving a challenge at its pinned URL may be charging somewhere else.

Method and data: meloliva14/x402-measure

To reproduce, from a clone:

```
python verify_snapshot.py --all
python monthly_report.py --end 2026-11-16
```

Every figure above, with its definition and the hash of each day it read, is in state_of_x402_2026-11.json beside this file.
